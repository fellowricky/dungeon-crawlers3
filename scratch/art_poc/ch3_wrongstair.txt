A close, unsettling scene at the point where a watchtower's rough cellar floor gives way to an ancient spiral stair descending into the dark. No people and no creatures appear in the scene. There is no readable writing of any kind. In the foreground, crude mortared fieldstone flooring — the tower's honest rustic masonry — breaks off at a black edge, and beyond it begins something entirely different: a spiral stair of flawless dressed ashlar, cut and fitted to an impossible tolerance, geometrically perfect, far older and far finer than anything the frontier could build. The joints are hairline-tight, the steps precise, and the stair curves down and down into a deep cold blackness that swallows a faint sourceless glow far below. The contrast between the two masonries is the whole point of the image. A guttering torch-glow from behind lights the near stone warm; the depths go cold blue-black. Palette of warm rough grey fieldstone against cold flawless pale ashlar and bottomless blue-black shadow. Mood of a threshold into something older and more precise than the world above it.
