A close scene of a small goblin camp struck in a panic and abandoned on open moorland, at grey dusk. No people and no creatures appear anywhere in the scene. In the foreground a cold cook-fire still ringed with blackened stones, a dented pot tipped over beside it, its spilled contents long dried. Crude hide bedrolls and lean-to shelters of stick and skin are left exactly where they lay, one collapsed; scattered gnawed bones, a dropped spear, a spilled sack of loot half-trodden into the mud — the disorder of people who ran without gathering anything. Nothing burned, no bodies. Around the camp the empty moor stretches off into low mist, tussock grass and grey outcrops under a bruised heavy sky. Muted palette of cold ash-grey, wet brown hide, dull green-grey moor and failing purple dusk light. Mood of a place fled from at a dead run — abandonment, not battle.
