A close, quiet scene of a small burned-out farmhouse standing roofless in a forest thicket, at cold overcast morning. No people and no creatures appear anywhere in the scene. The stone-and-timber cottage fills the centre: its thatch roof gone entirely to a lattice of charred black rafters open to the grey sky, walls scorched with soot-black tongues above every empty window, the doorframe a blackened maw. The fire is weeks old — cold, rain-washed, no smoke and no embers, pale ash settled and crusted, green weeds and nettles already pushing up through the burnt floor and doorsill. The place has been methodically stripped: the door hangs off one hinge, wall-pegs are bare where tools were lifted, but a low cellar hatch and a half-fallen pantry shelf still stand in shadow inside, hinting at things the looters missed. Damp pines crowd close around the ruin, mist between the trunks. Muted palette of charcoal black, wet grey stone, sodden brown ash and cold forest green. Mood of a home picked clean by something patient and organised, not desperate.
