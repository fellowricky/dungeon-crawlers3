A close view of a great ancient iron-bound vault door of dark timber and blackened iron, set into wet dressed stone, lit cold and dim. The door is clearly a century old and long shut — but the seal across its seam is jarringly fresh: a bright, glossy, sharp-edged blob of dark red sealing wax stamped with an abstract geometric guild sigil, and at the door's edge that fresh wax sits atop visible layers of older, duller wax seals stacked and scarred one over another, a whole history of reseals. The contrast between the ancient door and the brand-new seal over old ones is the subject.

Cold grey-blue light rakes the iron bands and the bright wax; deep black shadow around. Damp stone, a bead of water. No people. Palette of dark timber, black iron, wet grey stone, and the one hot red of fresh wax over dull old wax. Mood of a "sealed for a century" vault that is quietly opened and resealed on a schedule.
