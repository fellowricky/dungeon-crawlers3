A close, hushed interior scene of a long garrison mess table deep in a stone watchtower cellar, lit by a single dim shaft of grey light. No people appear anywhere in the scene. There is no readable writing of any kind. The heavy plank table runs down the centre of the frame, laid for sixteen and never cleared: tin plates, cups, a jug, wooden benches drawn up neatly on both sides, a paused game of cards spread mid-hand at the near end with a small pile of coins beside it. Every surface wears four years of thick, undisturbed grey dust; cobwebs bridge the cups and hang from the low vaulted ceiling. Not one bench is knocked over, nothing spilled or broken — the meal was simply left. Bare mortared fieldstone walls, a cold hearth in shadow, a narrow barred window high up admitting a single pale beam that falls across the dust. Palette of dusty grey stone and faded pewter against deep cold blue-black shadow, one shaft of wan daylight. Mood of interrupted ordinary life, frozen and abandoned mid-moment.
