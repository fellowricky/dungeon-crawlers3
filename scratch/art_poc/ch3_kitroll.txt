A close, eerie interior scene of a stone stair-landing at the top of a watchtower, where sixteen soldiers' kits are stacked against the wall with parade precision, dim cold light. No people appear anywhere in the scene. There is no readable writing of any kind. Along the wall, sixteen neat identical bundles of folded soldier's gear are stacked in a precise row — folded tunics, rolled belts, boots paired toe-to-toe, helmets set on top — every set squared away as if for inspection, as if the men undressed deliberately and walked away. On the topmost bundle lies a single captain's sash, a band of faded red cloth, set apart. Everything is furred with years of undisturbed dust. Bare ashlar walls, a worn stone stair curling up out of frame, a narrow arrow-slit letting in one thin blade of cold grey daylight. No struggle, no blood, nothing overturned. Palette of dusty grey stone and faded cloth against deep blue-black shadow, one cold pale shaft of light, the red sash the only warm note. Mood of a disciplined, willing, impossible disappearance.
