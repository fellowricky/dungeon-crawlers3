#WARM
A wide, warm, celebratory establishing shot of a great fortified guildhall courtyard at night, packed with a festival crowd, seen from a raised vantage looking down across the whole scene. This is a bright, prosperous, welcoming place — deliberately the opposite of a ruin. There is no written lettering of any kind anywhere.

The courtyard is enclosed by a large, handsome, intact stronghold of warm honey-coloured stone: sound walls with no cracks or damage, steep slate roofs, tall mullioned windows glowing golden from within, carved stone gables, and a broad flight of steps up to a grand open double door spilling warm light. Everything is in excellent repair, swept and maintained.

At the heart of the courtyard, slightly off-centre, roars an enormous open cook-fire the size of a small cottage — a huge crackling bonfire in a ringed stone pit with whole spitted carcasses turning over it, long trestle tables loaded with food and barrels ranged around it. It throws brilliant orange light and a storm of sparks up into the night.

The courtyard is thronged with a large crowd of medieval townsfolk and adventurers, all rendered very small, distant and indistinct — simple warm backlit silhouettes and loose impressionistic shapes at the scale of the wide shot, with no detailed faces anywhere. They cluster around the fire and tables, raising cups, clustered in knots, clearly celebrating. A few small figures dance.

Every single person in the crowd is dressed in strictly medieval peasant, artisan and adventurer clothing: loose belted wool tunics, hooded cloaks, long ankle-length skirts and kirtles, aprons, head-scarves and coifs, hose and cross-gartered leggings, soft leather boots, leather jerkins, the odd mail shirt or sword-belt. Silhouettes are loose, layered, draped and long. Absolutely nobody is wearing anything modern — no t-shirts, no shirts with printed designs, no jeans or trousers with modern cut, no shorts, no jackets with collars and zips, no caps or beanies, no trainers or sneakers, no backpacks, no eyeglasses, and no modern hairstyles. There are no modern objects anywhere in the courtyard.

Strings of lanterns and long lines of heraldic banners crisscross the courtyard overhead, hung between the buildings and from every window and parapet. The banners are rich solid-coloured cloth bearing simple bold abstract geometric heraldic devices — chevrons, discs, bars, lozenges — with absolutely no letters, no words and no writing on any of them.

Above, a clear deep blue-black night sky with cold stars, the warm firelight glow rising against it, and thin woodsmoke drifting off toward one corner.

Lighting is rich, golden and generous: the great bonfire as the dominant warm source, hundreds of lanterns and windows as smaller warm points, deep soft blue night shadow between them for contrast. Palette of glowing amber, orange and gold against warm honey stone and deep midnight blue. Mood of abundance, welcome, safety and loud good cheer — a genuinely happy place, with just a faint sense that it is all a great deal more than the company was promised.
