A deep underground stone landing at the bottom of a long descending stair, lit cold and low. Set out carefully on the worn flagstones, at the threshold of a dark doorway, is a garrison's pay, left as a deliberate offering: neat towers of stacked coins, service-rings threaded on a cord, bronze muster-tokens laid in rows, a captain's seal placed on top — arranged with care like coins laid on a temple step, freely given, untouched.

Beyond the offering, a dark doorway leads deeper into black. The masonry is flawless ancient dressed stone. A faint cold blue-white light rakes the landing; a weak warm gleam catches the gold of the stacked coins. No people, no bodies. Cold grey stone and black shadow against the one warm glint of the offered coin. Mood of a willing toll paid at the threshold of the dark.
