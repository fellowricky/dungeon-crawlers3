A dramatic low-angle boss-reveal portrait of a single small goblin chieftain enthroned inside a war-camp at night, seen from below so his throne looms over the viewer. Exactly one goblin is in focus; every other figure is only a distant dark silhouette.

The Goblin King is emphatically a goblin, and he is SMALL — a scrawny, wiry little creature barely the size of a human child, dwarfed by his own oversized throne, which he sits on cross-legged and hunched forward like a spoiled brat on a seat built for someone far bigger. His head is disproportionately large for his skinny body. He has enormous long pointed bat-like ears notched with scars, a squashed hooked nose, a wide toad-like mouth crowded with small crooked yellow teeth, and thin spindly arms and bandy legs with knobbly joints and long grasping fingers. His hide is dull sickly green. His small eyes glitter amber-orange with mean, gloating, petty cunning. Sparse black topknot hair, bone piercings, filthy skin. He is pathetic in body and terrifying only in attitude — a small nasty thing playing at being a king.

Everything he personally owns is squalid: stitched hide rags, filthy matted furs, knotted cord, a necklace of yellowed finger-bones, crude scrap-iron trinkets. A heavy notched cleaver of pitted rusty scrap iron lies across his lap, far too big for him to swing comfortably.

But his breastplate and one pauldron are the exception, and they are the focal point of the entire image. They are ancient dwarven deep-forged plate, and they must look unmistakably OLD and unmistakably ALIEN to this place — not shiny knight's armour. The metal is dark blue-black and gunmetal, deep and low in value with a soft satin sheen rather than a bright mirror polish, its edges worn smooth and round by many centuries of handling. Its whole surface is covered in severe, exact, deeply-cut geometric ornament: hard angular fluting, stepped chevrons, concentric bands and interlocking rectilinear key patterns, machined-precise and rigidly symmetrical, of a cold underground craftsmanship totally unlike the crude organic goblin junk around it. Thin lines of pale silver inlay run through the cut grooves and catch the light in fine bright threads against the dark metal. Despite its age it is flawless — no dents, no rust, no scratches. It is by far the finest, oldest and most perfectly made object anywhere in the picture, and it catches a crisp cool highlight that nothing else in the filthy camp does. It is scaled for a body three times his size: it swamps his little frame like a bell, hanging off his shoulders down past his hips, lashed on crookedly with rope and rawhide because it does not remotely fit. The contrast between the exquisite ancient armor and the grubby little creature wearing it is stark, deliberate and unmistakable — it is obviously stolen, obviously far older than anything else here, and obviously from somewhere very deep underground. It bears no letters or writing of any kind.

His throne is crude lashed timber, cart wheels, cracked shields and animal skulls, raised on trampled earth. The background is kept simple, dark and smoky: sharpened palisade stakes against the night, two or three tattered hide banners, a few guttering torches, and far back in the haze a handful of small hunched goblin silhouettes, faceless and indistinct.

Lighting is focused and controlled: warm orange torchlight rims his shoulders and skull from behind, while a cool clean highlight falls across the polished breastplate at the centre of the frame, making it the brightest and sharpest thing in the image. Deep black shadow, drifting embers, thin smoke. Mood of squalid petty menace with one detail that should not be there.
