#LANDSCAPE
A grim, quiet forest-floor scene at cold grey dawn. In the foreground, a single dead goblin lies sprawled face-down across a narrow dirt woodland trail, its body twisted as if it fell mid-stride at a dead run. One thin arm is flung out toward the viewer, its small clawed hands raw and torn from clawing through bramble in the dark. There is no wound and no blood on it — it simply ran until it dropped. Its bare feet point back the way it came, into the deep dark trees. A few pieces of dropped crude goblin gear — a notched knife, a cord of teeth — lie scattered where they fell.

The trail behind the body runs away into a wall of tall pine and oak trunks that recede into cold blue morning mist. Damp leaf litter, ferns, wet exposed roots. The light is flat, grey and shadowless. The mood is hushed and wrong — a small dead thing that died fleeing something worse than the party.

No other figures, no living creatures, no fire and no smoke. Muted palette of damp forest greens, brown leaf litter, wet grey bark and cold blue mist.
