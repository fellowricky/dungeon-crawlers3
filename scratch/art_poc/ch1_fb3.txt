A tight, atmospheric close-up of the lower half of an ancient grey granite standing stone in a small forest clearing at dusk. Crude goblin war-paint in ugly ochre-yellow and dried-blood red is smeared and daubed across the stone, now cracked and flaking and peeling away in patches.

Where the paint has flaked off, it reveals a deep-cut ancient geometric sigil carved into the granite beneath — precise interlocking angular lines and concentric rings, machine-exact and worn smooth by immense age, obviously far older and far finer than the crude paint slapped over it. This carved mark is a purely abstract geometric pattern, with no letters, no runes and no writing of any kind. A faint, cold, blue-white light seeps up out of the very deepest grooves of the carved sigil, the one cool glow in the scene, catching the damp edges of the stone.

Wet moss, grey lichen, dead leaves and a mushroom or two cluster at the stone's foot. The surrounding forest is dim and out of focus behind. Muted greys and forest greens against the sour ochre-red paint and the single cold blue glow. Mood of something old and sealed showing through a frightened scrawl.
