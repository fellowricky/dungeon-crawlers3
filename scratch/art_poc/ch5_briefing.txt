#LANDSCAPE
A wide, oppressive establishing shot of a drowned fenland under thick mist at dusk, looking out along a flooded causeway that vanishes into fog. No people are visible anywhere in the scene. There is no written lettering of any kind.

The whole landscape is black standing water — a vast, still, featureless flooded mire stretching out in every direction, its surface like dull black glass, broken only by reed beds, tussocks of dead sedge, and the skeletal remains of drowned trees. The dead trees stand in scattered clusters, bare bone-white and grey trunks with broken limbs, some leaning, some snapped off, all long dead and rotting at the waterline. Patches of scummy green weed and rafts of dark peat float on the surface.

Running from the immediate foreground into the middle distance is an ancient stone causeway, and its defining feature is that it is submerged: the water lies a hand's depth over the top of it, so the road reads only as a faint straight paler line of drowned flagstones just beneath the black surface, its edges lost. A few worn marker stones and the stubs of broken posts jut up out of the water along its length, leaning at angles, the only guide to where the road actually runs. On either side of it the water darkens immediately into bottomless peat.

Thick cold fog sits on the water and closes the scene down hard: visibility drops away to nothing about two thirds into the frame, swallowing the causeway completely and hiding whatever lies beyond. The fog is banked, layered and heavy, drifting in slow sheets, and it presses in from the sides of the frame as well, so the whole composition feels shut in and blind despite being an open landscape.

The sky above is a narrow band of cold dying dusk light — flat pewter grey with a thin sickly band of pale yellow-green at the horizon — reflected weakly in the black water below. No sun, no stars, no fires, no smoke.

Palette almost monochrome: black water, cold pewter and bone grey, drowned brown-olive reeds, dead white timber, with one faint sour yellow-green note in the sky and its reflection. Mood of cold, blind, patient hostility — a road that has been under water for a hundred years leading somewhere nobody should still be going.
