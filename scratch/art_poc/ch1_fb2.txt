#LANDSCAPE
A still, eerie, unnaturally silent deep-forest thicket at flat overcast midday, entirely empty of animals. In the foreground, an earthen bank holds the mouth of a badger sett that has been torn open from the inside — clawed outward and upward through its own roof, fresh dark earth flung out across the leaf litter, as if whatever lived there fled straight up out of the ground rather than out the entrance.

Nearby in the ferns lies a fallen bird's nest, tipped on its side, a clutch of pale eggs gone cold and abandoned in it. On the dark tree trunks rising behind, deep parallel claw-marks rake the bark unnaturally high overhead — squirrel, marten and bird scratches gouged as high up the trunks as the lower branches, the whole treeful of animals having fled upward as far as it could climb.

Dense dark pine and oak, hanging grey-green moss, cold gloom, a few weak shafts of grey light. Wet fallen leaves. The mood is of a place abruptly and completely evacuated from below. No animals, no people, no fire, no smoke. Muted cold greens, damp browns, grey bark.
