A close, torchlit night-time detail of a section of crude goblin palisade wall. Rough-hewn, sharpened timber stakes are lashed together — but not with rope. They are bound with lengths of fine, dark, close-forged iron chain: precise, even, old smith-work links of a quality no goblin ever made, wound and knotted crudely around the raw timber, looped from stake to stake and hung here and there with a few crude skulls, feathers and bone fetishes. The chain is obviously scavenged treasure being used carelessly as cordage — beautiful ancient ironwork knotted around firewood.

Warm orange torchlight from one side rakes hard across the cold gleaming iron links and the rough splintered wood, throwing deep black shadow into the gaps behind the stakes. Gritty, weathered, grimy. The framing is tight on the join where the chain wraps the timber, so the mismatch between the fine iron and the crude wood is the whole subject.

No people, no faces, no creatures. Muted browns and black with hot orange torch-glow and the cold hard gleam of forged iron.
