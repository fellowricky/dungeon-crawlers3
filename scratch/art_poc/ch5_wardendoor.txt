A close, grim scene of a single dead sentinel still standing guard before a great vault door in a drowned undercroft, dim cold light. Exactly one motionless armoured figure, seen from the front, faces the viewer — a long-dead vault warden that has not left its post. Its plate breastplate is charter-issue, battered with three deep dents, the collar-sigil still just visible; the body inside is withered and mummified in its corroded armour, gauntleted hands still closed on the haft of a grounded polearm, head bowed under a rusted helm, cobwebbed and furred with the dust and slime of long years. It stands directly before a massive iron-bound stone vault door, sealed with a heavy wax sigil that matches the mark at its collar. The undercroft around it is wet and cold: dripping vaulted stone, black water skinning the flagstones, mildew and hanging weed. A single shaft of sickly grey-green light from above falls on the standing corpse and the great door. Careful correct anatomy, one figure only, no extra limbs. Palette of corroded grey steel, drowned green-black stone and one cold shaft of wan light. Mood of undying, pointless duty — a guard that death did not relieve.
