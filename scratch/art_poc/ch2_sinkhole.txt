A dramatic downward view into an enormous circular sinkhole that has opened in a bleak grassy moor, looking down over its crumbling lip into the darkness below. No people are visible anywhere in the scene.

The mouth of the pit is roughly forty feet across and fills most of the frame, ringed by torn turf, hanging roots, and slabs of collapsed sod at the rim. The immediate foreground is the broken grassy edge the viewer stands on.

The critical detail is the shaft wall: it is not earth. Below the thin skin of turf and peat, the sides of the pit are precisely cut dressed stone — huge fitted rectangular ashlar blocks in regular courses, joints tight, faces worn smooth, marching down into the dark. It is unmistakably built, deliberate, ancient masonry, far older and far finer than anything on the surface, and it descends further than the light reaches. A few courses are cracked and shoved crookedly outward.

Crucially, the debris pattern shows the collapse went the wrong way. Broken stone, shattered blocks and clods of earth are flung UP and OUTWARD onto the surrounding grass around the rim rather than fallen inward, and the outward-shoved masonry makes it clear that something forced its way up and out of the shaft rather than the ground caving in.

At the bottom of the pit, dimly lit and partly lost in shadow, lies a great heaped tangle of dead cattle — dozens of pale broken bodies piled and twisted where they fell, horns and stiff legs jutting at wrong angles, half-buried in rubble. Carrion flies and a few black birds. The pile is rendered at a distance, murky and indistinct rather than gory.

Below and beyond the cattle the shaft continues down into total blackness, a throat of dressed stone going somewhere much deeper.

Cold grey daylight from above falls in a hard circle on the upper courses of stonework and the rim grass, fading fast into deep blue-black shadow below. Drifting dust. Palette of wet grey stone, drab moor green, peat brown and the pale sick colour of dead cattle, against heavy black depth. Mood of vertigo, wrongness and a deep place opened from the inside.
