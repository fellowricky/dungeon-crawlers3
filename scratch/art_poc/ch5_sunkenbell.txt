A close, melancholy scene of a great bronze church-bell lying fallen in reeds beside a chapel drowned to its rafters in black fen-water, at cold grey dusk. No people and no creatures appear anywhere in the scene. There is no readable writing of any kind. In the foreground the huge weathered bell lies mouth-up in the shallows, half-sunk in mud and reed, its bowl brimming with still black water and green slime, its bronze gone verdigris-green and streaked, a faint patina of old consecration still clinging to it. Behind it, the flooded chapel stands only as a broken gable and a few drowned stone arches and rafter-beams rising out of the black mere, ivy and dead reed hanging from them, mist coiling low over the water. A single shaft of wan grey light breaks the overcast and touches the wet bronze. Everything is water-logged, still, funereal. Palette of verdigris green, black water, drowned grey stone and cold pale sky. Mood of drowned holiness — something sacred sunk but not yet gone out.
